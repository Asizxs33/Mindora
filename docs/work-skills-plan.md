# Mindora · Two breaks, one weekly report

## Who needs it

Start with US operations coordinators aged 25–45 who prepare a weekly expense update while balancing shifts and family commitments. The immediate job is to turn a few receipts into a reliable total and a short message to a manager. An interrupted break can be the whole study session. The first five minutes should produce work someone can check.

Across participating OECD countries, 48% of adults who experienced barriers to adult learning cited time constraints related to work or family as their main barrier. This supports the scheduling problem. Demand for this particular offer among US coordinators still needs direct testing. [OECD, Trends in Adult Learning](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/trends-in-adult-learning_ec0624a6-en/full-report/why-is-participation-not-more-common_73893b53.html)

## Guest walkthrough

Open [the two-week exercise](https://mindora-livid-eta.vercel.app/#weekly-report). Day two is available immediately after the guided report so a reviewer can test the whole journey in one sitting. A learner can also close the browser and return to the same URL on the same browser profile. Guest drafts stay in that browser. Signed-in drafts use the same account workspace as the rest of the learning path.

| When | Learner action | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Today, first minute | Read the Friday request from the manager. Identify expense amounts in column B and the $100 budget. | Three expense rows of $24, $38 and $18. |
| Today, next three minutes | Use the worked range, total the rows and decide whether the team stayed within budget. | The app checks the range, total and decision separately. |
| Today, last minute | Read the resulting manager update. Stop for the day or open the next week. | Week one totals $80, or $20 under budget. |
| Tomorrow, first minute | Recall how to identify the first and last expense cells. Inspect the new delivery, supplies, printing and packing figures. | The exercise opens without a filled-in answer. |
| Tomorrow, next three minutes | Adapt the range for four expenses. Work out the total and make the budget decision independently. | $16 + $42 + $27 + $18 = $103. The old range misses packing. |
| Tomorrow, last minute | Read the checked report and explain the change to a manager. | The team is now $3 over budget. The view retains both checked reports on the same browser. |

Retrieval and spaced practice inform the design. Testing whether busy adults actually return and transfer the skill is a separate product experiment. [IES practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/PracticeGuide/1/Published)

## Offer and economics

One personal AI course is free, including its lessons and saved progress. Public examples remain open to reviewers. The planned Plus price for additional personal courses is **$9.99 a month or $99.99 a year**. Twelve monthly payments total $119.88. Paying annually saves $19.89 (16.6%) and works out to $8.33 per month. Paid enrollment awaits a billing provider.

The team reported a maximum observed token bill of **$1.78 for one ten-course batch**. The model below assumes one paying subscriber generates ten courses every month at that same total token cost, including under the annual plan. Actual monthly usage and the cost of later lessons still need measurement.

| USD per subscriber | One monthly payment | One year paid annually |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Subscription revenue at list price | $9.99 | $99.99 |
| Modelled token spend | $1.78 | $21.36 |
| Revenue left after tokens | $8.21 | $78.63 |
| Net profit required for a 63% net margin | $6.29 | $62.99 |
| Budget left for **all other costs** at that margin | $1.92 | $15.64 |

The 63% figure is a planning scenario. At that target, the annual plan allows about $1.30 per month for all costs beyond tokens. The calculation uses unrounded prices and rounds the displayed results to cents. Check the model against recognized revenue after discounts and refunds, payment fees, free-course usage, retries, hosting, support, acquisition, allocated operating expenses and applicable taxes. Verified net profit requires actual revenue and expenses.

At list prices, annual recurring revenue is $119.88 per active monthly subscriber and $99.99 per active annual subscriber. A $100M ARR scenario therefore needs about 834,168 paying subscribers on monthly plans, 1,000,101 on annual plans or 909,629 with an even split by subscriber count. These figures describe the scale required. Measure acquisition spend per new payer (CAC), contribution after servicing costs and paid customer lifetime by billing plan before claiming LTV exceeds CAC.

## What to measure next

Recruit US operations coordinators with a real Friday report due. Observe whether they finish one report in a short break, then return the following day to produce the second report without help. The report now saves each completion time and the number of checks used. Compare the learner’s local calendar days to distinguish an immediate preview from later-day practice. For a consented pilot, count unique learners who finish day one, return the next day and finish day two on their first check. Report the denominator and record early previews separately. Guest results stay on their device. Ask what they used previously and how long their actual task took. After checkout opens, measure paid conversion from the second-course offer, repeat usage, cancellations and full variable cost per paying subscriber. Report counts and denominators with each result.
